What Do All These Numbers Mean?

The Easy Metrics (Like Test Scores)

Test Accuracy (0.70 to 0.90):
This is like getting answers right on a test. If accuracy is 0.80, your computer got 8 out of 10 guesses correct!
Your results: Pretty good! Most models got 7-9 out of 10 right.
ROC-AUC (0.50 to 1.00):
This is a special score that tells us how good your computer is at telling the difference between "drug works" and "drug doesn't work."
• 0.50 = Random guessing (like flipping a coin)
• 0.70 = Pretty good
• 0.80+ = Really good!
Your results: Most of your models scored 0.80+, which is great!
CV Mean & CV Std:
This tells us if your computer gives the same answer when we test it multiple times.
• Low Std = Consistent (like always getting similar test scores)
• High Std = Inconsistent (like sometimes getting A's, sometimes getting C's)
Your results: A bit inconsistent, but not too bad.

What Does "Precision, Recall, F1-Score" Mean?

Imagine you're looking for red balls in a big box of mixed colored balls:
• Precision: Of all the balls you picked as "red," how many were actually red?
• Recall: Of all the actual red balls in the box, how many did you find?
• F1-Score: A combination score that balances both precision and recall

What About Predicting Drug Amounts (Regression)?

R² (R-squared): This tells us how well your computer can predict the exact amount of drug needed.
• 1.0 = Perfect predictions
• 0.5 = Okay predictions
• 0.0 or negative = Really bad predictions

Your results: The regression models didn't work well (negative R² values), which means they're worse than just guessing the average. This is like trying to guess someone's exact height and being worse than just saying "average height" for everyone.

The Bottom Line

Good News: Your computer learned to predict if drugs work pretty well!

Best Model: Logistic Regression - use this one for predicting if drugs work.

Why These Results Happened

Possible Problems:
  • Small Dataset: You only have 100 samples, which is like trying to learn about all dogs by only seeing 100 dogs - not enough!
  • Complex Relationships: Gene data is really complicated, and the relationships between genes and drug response might be too complex for simple models.
  • Data Quality: Maybe the genes you're looking at aren't the right ones for predicting drug response.

What Should You Do Next?

  1. Use your Logistic Regression model for predicting if drugs work (yes/no questions)
  2. Don't use the regression models for predicting drug amounts - they're not working
  3. Get more data if possible - at least 500-1000 samples would be much better
  4. Try different genes that might be more related to drug response
  5. Be careful about the SVM model - it might be overfitting (memorizing instead of learning)
